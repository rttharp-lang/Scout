## REJECTED — full reasoning

### R1. Agent 03 SIGNAL 4 — "Nike publishes men's inseams by the inch and withholds women's"
**Rejected as stated.** Nike's **own 2026 Women's Basketball team catalogue** publishes the Digital DF Layup Short at **6.25" inseam (all sizes)**, **21.5" hip width (M)**, with **+1" / +2" / −0.75" stature grading**. The narrower finding that survives: **men's SKUs are *named* by the inch and women's DTC copy substitutes "optimal inseam length."** That is a merchandising-copy asymmetry, and it is a legitimate observation. It is not information asymmetry, and it cannot carry the "women's line treated as second-class information" weight Agent 03 put on it. Agent 03 marked the women's inseam "not retrieved"; I retrieved it in one search.

Caveat on my own evidence: I checked a **navigation facet**, not a garment measurement. What is falsified is the assertion that high rise is absent from the category's merchandising vocabulary. Whether adidas's high-rise basketball product is meaningfully high-rise is a teardown question.

### R4. Agent 07 GAP A — "5" is empty in men's basketball / men's retail bottoms out around 8""
**Rejected by Agent 03's own teardown table in the same study**, which lists **Nike Dri-FIT 5" Mesh Basketball Shorts**, **Standard Issue 5" Practice Mesh**, **Standard Issue 5" Practice Woven**, and **DNA 4" 2-in-1**. Foot Locker operates a dedicated **"5 Inch Basketball Shorts"** nav category. Nike Team's men's base is 8" with a −0.75" grade to 7.25". This is a direct intra-study contradiction on a checkable fact and Agent 07 is on the wrong side of it.

### R5. Agent 07's defensibility column — "piping/taping: no function, not defensible"
**Rejected.** adidas holds **24 US federal registrations** on stripe variations covering apparel, has litigated stripes running down pant legs (Juicy), won a **$305M** jury award against Payless/Collective Brands, and is currently suing Fashion Nova and Aviator Nation. A purely decorative applied stripe is the **most valuable and most aggressively defended design signature in athletic apparel**, and it has been carried on shorts for seventy years.

Agent 07 conflated **"has no engineering function"** with **"cannot be owned."** Those are different axes. Collapsing them is what allows its central thesis to reach its conclusion.

### R6. Agent 07's central thesis — "no signature exists because every advance is invisible and unphotographable"
**Rejected as a causal explanation. It survives only as a description of the current state.** Three independent counter-examples:

1. **adidas three-stripes** — visible, functionless, ferociously defensible, on shorts, at global scale, for decades. A visible signature was always available; it required no engineering at all.
2. **Eric Emanuel** — the most commercially potent signature basketball short of the era, built on applied logo + colour + scarcity, protected by trademark, with active counterfeiting litigation. Agent 07 cites EE as proof the short can be the whole product, then constructs a thesis that rules out the mechanism EE actually used.
3. **Nike's own Aero-FIT running, 2026** — "the lighter-coloured mesh zones are **intentionally visible** to show runners where the extra breathability lives." Nike deliberately made invisible engineering visible, on a short, this year. The thesis that this cannot be done is disproved by Nike having just done it.

The category's lack of a construction-led signature is a **strategic choice**, not a physical constraint. That is a more useful diagnosis for a PLM reader, and it changes the recommendation: the question is not "how do we make engineering visible" (Nike knows how) but "why does basketball keep choosing the decal."

## PLAUSIBLE — NEEDS VALIDATION — with settling test

**P1. The WNBA body-map programme (Agent 07 Signal 3, OPP-D1, OPP-D4).**
Features and quote verified verbatim. **Date now hard: April 2021.** The "Nike built it and buried it" asset is **five years and four months old**. Agent 07 flagged exactly this risk and was right to.
*Settling test (both internal, both fast):* (a) Does the 2025/2026 WNBA uniform chassis still use body-mapped closed-hole mesh, or has it been superseded? (b) Has any of that pattern engineering flowed into consumer women's basketball product? Agent 07 called (b) "the single most checkable claim in the report." It is.

**P2. OPP-D1 aperture map, reclassified.**
Not a novel proposition — a port of Nike's own fall-2026 running Aero-FIT visible-zone language into basketball. That makes it *more* actionable and *less* original.
*Settling test:* cost-per-unit and knit-machine cycle time on the running Aero-FIT short versus a DNA mesh short. This is the question Agent 07 itself identified as decisive ("signature or capsule?") and it is answerable from internal costing, not research.

**P3. Agent 03 OPPORTUNITY D (DRYV moisture panel).**
Patent verified: **US 8,769,716**, granted 8 July 2014. Agent 03 was right to demand clearance. What it missed: a US utility patent granted in 2014 typically runs ~20 years from earliest non-provisional filing — **this is live into roughly the early 2030s**, a real block, not a formality. It is also a **US grant**; foreign coverage is unretrieved, so an ex-US route may exist. And nobody has read the **claims**, which is the only thing that determines scope.
*Settling test:* freedom-to-operate opinion on the claim set of US 8,769,716 and its family, plus an international family search. Until that exists, Opportunity D is unfalsifiable in the way that matters commercially.

**P4. Agent 07 OPP-D3 (short inseam × wide leg opening).**
Half-confirmed, half-unfalsifiable. **Confirmed:** leg opening is genuinely not published at consumer retail — I found no leg-opening spec for any basketball short from any brand. **Not confirmed:** that the quadrant is empty. Also, Nike Team **does** publish **hip width** (23" men's L, 21.5" women's M), so "width is never specced" is too strong.
*Settling test:* physical teardown — measure leg opening, hip width, rise and inseam on ~20 SKUs across Nike / adidas / UA / POINT 3 / Eric Emanuel at 5"/7"/9"/11". Agent 07 prescribed this itself. Do not promote OPP-D3 above "hypothesis" until it is done.

**P5. Agent 03 SIGNAL 2 / OPPORTUNITY C (mesh transparency and durability).**
Undated reviews, no sample size, no return-rate data, US-only. And now a confound Agent 03 could not see: **Dri-FIT Icon, the named concentration point of the thinness and transparency complaints, is the cheapest rung on the ladder at $34 / $29.99.** Complaints about thinness clustering on the cheapest SKU is what price predicts, not what a category-wide mesh failure predicts.
*Settling test:* internal return-reason codes and warranty claims **normalised by unit volume and by price tier**, by SKU. If Icon's complaint rate is flat against DNA at 1.8x the price, the problem is mesh. If it scales with price, Opportunity C is chasing a cost decision, not a technology gap.

**P6. Aero-FIT's ">2x airflow."**
Brand claim, no independent verification. Correctly flagged by Agent 03 and left flagged.
*Settling test:* independent air-permeability and thermal-manikin testing versus Dri-FIT ADV, with the test protocol published. Nike will need this anyway the first time a competitor challenges the claim.

**P7. Agent 07 OPP-D4 (women's basketball short designed as basketball).**
The strongest surviving opportunity in Agent 07, and it is now better-supported than Agent 07 knew: Nike Team already publishes a **women's-specific 6.25" base inseam with its own stature grading** and the WNBA programme built female-specific pattern engineering in 2021. *Settling test:* same as P1(b). One afternoon of internal line review.

## INDEPENDENT CORROBORATION vs SHARED-SOURCE ECHO (challenge 9 — decisive)

**Ruling: on zoned/body-mapped mesh, this is ONE VOICE, not two signals. Do not count it twice.**

- **Agent 03's** zoned-mesh evidence: about.nike.com Aero-FIT release + about.nike.com Sabrina 4 release + Forbes, which is a recap of the Nike release.
- **Agent 07's** zoned-mesh evidence: wnba.com + WWD + CBS, all coverage of the Nike WNBA uniform launch.

Every strand is **Nike marketing copy**. Neither agent read a non-Nike technical source, neither has a third-party test, neither has product in hand. On the proposition "Nike does body-mapped venting," this is Nike telling the story twice.

**But the nuance matters and the orchestrator should hold it precisely:** these are **two different press cycles about two different programmes, five years apart, in two different categories** — the 2021 WNBA Dri-FIT ADV closed-hole mesh, and the 2025–26 Aero-FIT elliptical open/closed knit. The agents are not citing the same document.

**So: one source *type*, one *voice*, two independent *programmes*.** The correct read is — **strong evidence that Nike holds the capability and has now demonstrated it twice; zero independent evidence that the capability delivers the claimed benefit.** Weight the capability high. Weight the performance claim low.

**A second, worse echo the orchestrator must catch.** **Agents 02 and 03 both assert Aero-FIT is "confined to one athlete's capsule."** Both are reading the same Nike Sabrina 4 page. Neither found the Aero-FIT running page. Their agreement is **not** confirmation — it is **the same missing search made twice**, and it has already propagated into Agent 02's Opportunity A and Agent 03's Tension T2. Two agents agreeing on an error is a correlated failure, not corroboration.

**One genuinely independent corroboration exists, and it is not the one the study leans on.** **Agent 05** sources the waistband-rolling behaviour from independent reporting — noting it is "especially popular in women's basketball," with hem-tucking under the compression layer as the most common women's modification. ## EVIDENCE vs ARTICULATE ASSERTION (my ruling on Agent 07)

**Blunt ruling: roughly 25–30% of Agent 07 is evidence. The rest is craft judgement.**

The arithmetic. Seven searches, zero fetches. Of nine signals: **three are properly sourced and hold** (3, 5, 9 — and 9 I upgraded beyond what Agent 07 claimed for it). **Two are thin to poor** (7 is footwear-only and says nothing about shorts; 8 rests on replica-vendor and SEO sites). **The rest are retailer SEO buying guides** (1, 2) **or brand self-description** (6).
