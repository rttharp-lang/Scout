## REJECTED — FULL REASONING

### R1. Agent 02 Signal 1 — "Nike's apparel business shrank in FY26 while adidas apparel grew 20%. This is the single largest fact in the domain."

Both halves fail.

**Nike half.** The quoted sentence — *"Apparel revenues decreased 7% on a currency-neutral basis in fiscal 2026. Unit sales of apparel decreased 8%..."* — matches neither FY26 nor FY25 as I retrieved them. FY26: apparel **+5% CN, units +5%, ASP flat**, and NIKE Brand apparel dollars **$13.0B → $13.4B**. FY25: apparel **-9% CN, units -6%, ASP -3pts** — with **-8% belonging to footwear units**. Agent 02's sentence is a splice of fragments from at least two different lines, plausibly two different years. The $13.4B figure reconciles cleanly against two independently verified totals ($45.2B NIKE Brand, $46.4B Inc.), so I am confident in the direction: **Nike apparel grew in FY26.**

**adidas half.** +20% apparel is **Q4 2025**. Full-year 2025 apparel was **+15%**.

**Why this matters more than the numbers.** Agent 02's read-through was: *"Nike lost apparel units (-8%) and held revenue only via mix. That is the signature of a brand trading down volume for ASP — the exact opposite of what a shorts franchise needs."* The corrected data says the **precise inverse**: Nike grew apparel units ~5% while ASP stayed flat because it was *"offset primarily by higher discounts and channel mix."* Nike bought units with price.

That inversion is the one place where correcting a figure makes another finding *stronger*: **the corrected 10-K language is near-direct total-apparel corroboration of Agent 04's clearance-pressure observation.** The evidence Agent 02 used to build an ASP-mix story actually supports Agent 04's discount story. Anyone consolidating these reports must not carry Agent 02's read-through forward.

### R2. Agent 02 — "The premium tier is empty; no $80+ basketball short surfaced."

Falsified four independent ways, three of them **available inside this corpus on the same day**:
- **Nike LeBron Men's 8" Basketball Shorts, $90 regular, live on Nike.com** (my verification).
- **adidas Crazylight, $80** — in **Agent 04's own markdown table**, observed at Foot Locker on 2026-08-30.
- **NOCTA Lightweight Basketball Shorts, $90** — Agent 03.
- **Nike Sportswear Circa 72, $95** — Agent 03.

This was never an evidence gap. It was a search-coverage gap that a second agent had already closed before Agent 02 wrote the claim. Agent 02 labeled it "an absence-of-evidence finding, not proof of absence," which is honest — but then built **Opportunity A** on it anyway as if it were established whitespace.

**And the correction inverts the strategic meaning.** The premium tier is not empty; it is **occupied and failing to hold price**. The $90 LeBron sits at $56.97 (-37%) on Nike.com with a further 25% member discount stackable — potentially **~-53%**. adidas's $80 Crazylight is at -40%. That is not whitespace to enter at $65–85. That is a demonstrated inability to hold $80–90 for a basketball short. Agent 02's own proposed guardrail — *"hold it out of the Nike.com markdown cadence"* — is precisely the discipline Nike is currently failing to apply to the $90 short it already sells.

### R3. Agent 02 Signal 2 — "Nike's price architecture is inverted: DTC is discounting while wholesale holds MSRP."

Comparing a sale page to a browse page and concluding the DTC channel discounts more deeply is comparing a discount surface to a non-discount surface.

The falsification is inside the corpus: **Agent 04 pulled from `footlocker.com/category/sport/basketball/sale/...` and found Foot Locker discounting the same styles** — DNA 24 -22%, DNA 8" -25%, LeBron -25%, Jordan Brooklyn -18%. Foot Locker is not holding MSRP; Agent 02 simply didn't query its sale page.

A narrow remnant survives: on the LeBron 8", Nike.com (-37% + stackable 25%) is deeper than Foot Locker (-25%). One item is not an architecture.

## THE MARKDOWN INFERENCE — MY VERDICT

**It does not survive.** Not as an inference about inseam, and not as evidence of over-investment. It survives only as what Agent 04 finally called it: a target list for internal verification.

Agent 04 disclosed the missing baseline honestly and repeatedly. That disclosure is correct but insufficient, because there are **four independent defects**, any one of which is fatal, and Agent 04 named only the first.

### Defect 1 — Selection on the dependent variable (fatal, unnamed by Agent 04)

Nine of the thirteen rows were harvested from pages **defined by being marked down**: `nike.com/w/sale-basketball-shorts`, `footlocker.com/.../sale/...`, `goinggoinggone.com/f/clearance-...`, `kohls.com/catalog/clearance-...`.

A sample drawn from clearance pages is **100% marked down by construction.** You cannot infer *which styles are disproportionately marked down* from a sample in which every item is marked down, because there is no unmarked-down comparison group and no denominator. This is not a missing-control problem that a prior-year baseline would fix — it is a sample that cannot answer the question at all.

This defect also silently destroys the second half of the hypothesis, which is worse than Agent 04 admits. Agent 04 says the 6" under-supply read rests on "absence of clearance." But **a 6" short selling at full price is invisible to a clearance sample by construction.** The absence of 6" carries literally zero information. And the corpus confirms 6" SKUs exist in volume: DNA 6", Standard Issue 6" Reversible, Standard Issue 5", Kobe 6" Standard Issue Reversible, KB Dri-FIT Fund 6", Nike Attack 5" (W), Crossover 5" (W) — plus **LeBron DNA Men's 6" 3-in-1**, which surfaced in my own search today.

### Defect 2 — The descriptive claim fails on Agent 04's own table

"13 markdowns concentrated in 10"/11"" is, on inspection, **three long-inseam observations, one of which points the other way, and two of which may be the same product.**

**(a) Is $9.72 vs $50 over-investment or normal clearance?** Normal clearance, and probably not even that — see Defect 4.
