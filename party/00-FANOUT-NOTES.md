# PHASE 1 — FAN OUT (condensed record)

Eight specialists ran in parallel. What follows is the compressed intelligence,
kept because Phase 2 depends on knowing which insights arrived independently.

## THE CONVERGENCES (the only part that matters)

Seven of eight agents were briefed separately and could not see each other's work.
These are the places where they independently landed on the same idea. Convergence
under isolation is the strongest signal a fan-out can produce.

**01 · The guests' own objects beat any decor budget.**
Cultural Scout proposed a "trade-in door" (Corteiz's Bolo Exchange — admission by
bringing, not receiving). Experience Architect independently proposed a "walk-in
gauntlet" of unlabelled objects and named its real function: *status scramble* —
when the objects come from across the org's history, the junior person who knows
one becomes the most interesting person in the room. Memory Designer ranked "The
Archive Table" and "The Last Pair" in the top six. Three agents, one conclusion:
**put the team's own material in the room and get out of the way.**

**02 · Personalize what people already own. Never manufacture new swag.**
Cultural Scout: the "services row" — chainstitch embroiderer, sign painter, barber
altering things you walked in wearing. Memory Designer ranked "The Patch Table" #2
of ten. Food + Object Curator arrived at the same law from economics: *"An object's
value is set by the transaction that delivers it, not by the object."*

**03 · The leader does one small physical thing. No microphone, ever.**
Tequila Director's single strongest ritual: the host, not a bartender, pours the
flight. Branding's Morgue File territory: the host hangs one tag of their own.
Memory Designer: forty seconds at the box. Experience Architect's anti-pattern #1:
*"The instant a mic exists, the party becomes an audience and the host becomes
management."*

**04 · One unannounced state change, around minute 70–78.**
Experience Architect put "the unannounced shift" at 70. Music Director put a
percussionist walking in from the crowd at 78. Memory Designer's #10 was "The Room
That Changes Once." Nobody coordinated this.

**05 · Nothing may require a guest to read an instruction.**
Experience Architect anti-pattern #2. Producer's Law 4. Branding solved it by making
all signage *look* like archive labels rather than directions.

**06 · The record of the night should arrive later, not that night.**
Cultural Scout: portraits mailed as a bound set; the DJ set released the following
week. Branding's Press Row: film developed live on a wire. Food + Object: "delay"
listed as one of four levers that make an object worth keeping.

## THE SINGLE BEST LINE FROM EACH AGENT

- **01 Cultural Scout** — "60 people who design basketball apparel for a living have better taste than any decor budget. Programming that surfaces *their* objects is unfakeable, unbranded, and structurally impossible for HR to have produced."
- **02 Creative Director** — Three territories: PRESS ROW (the night covered like a game), THE LOW POST (candlelit agave room inside a painted key), THE MORGUE FILE (a wake for killed product, played straight).
- **03 Experience Architect** — "A room that gets more interesting the longer you stay in it." Plus: *salt the room* — twelve people inside with drinks before doors, which deletes the awkward first twenty minutes.
- **04 Tequila Director** — "Nobody says the word 'tequila' in the room. The bar simply behaves like a bar that knows things."
- **05 Music Director** — "Volume, not tempo, does the steering." 66dB at the door climbing to 94dB at minute 95, then a hard stop at 119 — never a fade, because a fade is an instruction to leave.
- **06 Food + Object** — "Give the object a job during the party. By the time they reach the door it isn't a favor, it's theirs, and taking it home isn't a decision."
- **07 Memory Designer** — The grammar of a two-hour party is **ambient · punctuation · terminal**. Three anchors maximum; at four, guests narrate the event instead of the moment and the one-sentence test fails.
- **08 Producer** — "Two hours is really 75 minutes." And: "Buy what they touch, not what they look at."
