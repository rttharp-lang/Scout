# 01 · SAMPLE SIZE

**02 THE IDEA**
Sixty people each bring one pair of their own shoes, and for two hours the room is a
lit museum of the team's own taste — every pair on a plinth, every plinth with a
hand-lettered card naming the owner, the place, the year and one line about why.

**03 WHY IT WORKS**
It solves the hardest problem in work-party design — *how do sixty people who see each
other daily talk to each other differently* — with a mechanism rather than a program.
The moment a beat-up pair from a specific 2011 afternoon is lit like an accession
object, the person who owns it holds court, regardless of title. Status scrambles by
itself. And the decor budget is zero, because the guests **are** the decor: sixty
people whose profession is taste, each contributing their single best object. No
agency could fake this room and no other company could stage it.

The second engine is provenance. A single-estate tequila labelled by rancho and vintage
and a pair of shoes labelled by city and year are the *same card*. That equivalence is
the whole identity, and it means the bar, the food, the walls and the guests are all
speaking one sentence.

**04 THE WORLD**
Bone-white walls, a grid of low black plinths under tight warm spots, everything else
dark. It smells like nixtamalized corn off a plancha and the cold citrus of the door
tray. It sounds like Azymuth at conversational volume, then Fela, then Crystal Waters.
The only handwriting in the room is one sign painter's, working a small table all night
with a brush and a rack of drying cards. It feels like a gallery that forgot to be
quiet.

**05 THE HERO MOMENT — THE LAST PLINTH**
One plinth stands lit and empty from the first minute, its card blank on the letterer's
table. Nobody explains it. At roughly minute 100 the host walks over, takes off the
shoes they arrived in, sets them on it, says one line to the letterer, and walks back
into the party in socks. No microphone. No gathering. The room notices in waves, which
is better than all at once.

**06 THE 120-MINUTE ARC** — see Phase 6.

**07 TEQUILA**
Additive-free, single-estate only. A pre-poured arrival coupe at the threshold so a
drink is in hand within four seconds. Two batched, pre-diluted cocktails at six seconds
a pour. A back bar hung like a gallery — fourteen bottles, labels forward, four inches
apart. The host pours the neat flight themselves.

**08 MUSIC** — Spiritual jazz at 66dB climbing to 94dB at minute 95. A percussionist
walks in from the crowd at minute 78. Hard stop at 119, never a fade.

**09 FOOD** — One taco, three times, the corn changing at 0:15, 0:50 and 1:25, each
sleeve printed with the corn's name and the elevation it grew at. The same card, in
your hand, edible.

**10 BRAND IDENTITY** — The provenance card. One ruled 3×5 format applied at every
scale from a napkin to an eight-foot wall.

**11 ENVIRONMENT** — 61 plinths, a letterer's table, a gallery-hung bar, a plancha
counter, no chairs for more than a third of the room.

**12 PHOTOGRAPHABLE** — The plinth grid, shot down the row, sixty pairs receding.

**13 TAKEAWAY** — Your card. Hand-lettered, one of one, retrieved when you collect
your shoes.

**14 COST** — $$ to $$$. The lowest decor spend of the three concepts.

**15 COMPLEXITY** — MEDIUM. Plinths and lighting are the only build. The letterer is
one person.

**16 WHAT COULD GO WRONG** — Guests forget to bring a pair, and feel exposed. Fixed by
eight house plinths and a letterer briefed to card anything a guest is wearing.

# 02 · THE MORGUE FILE

**02 THE IDEA** A one-night archive of the team's killed work — every dead project on a
tagged garment bag, hung on a rail, because the work that dies is the work you can
finally talk about.

**03 WHY IT WORKS** It is the most original idea produced in the entire fan-out and the
only one with genuine emotional risk in it. It converts institutional grief into black
comedy and shared relief, and it gives sixty people permission to say out loud what
they have only said in DMs.

**04 THE WORLD** Cold overhead fluorescent in the archive zone dropping to near-black
warmth at the bar — the temperature shift is the entire trick. Stainless, clear poly,
manila, brass grommets, rubber floor. Ambient dub techno from behind the shelving,
warming as the room thaws.

**05 THE HERO MOMENT** At minute 60 the host hangs a tag of their own — a project they
lost, named out loud, in under a minute — and then it's a party again.

**07 TEQUILA** The menu is a clipboard chart: `NO. / CONTENTS / STATUS`. Three read
`APPROVED`. One reads `KILLED`. The killed one is the best drink in the building and
it's available on request.

**08 MUSIC** No visible DJ. A selector works behind a rack, unbilled.

**09 FOOD** Stainless trays in strict grid formation, precisely portioned, genuinely
excellent. Order as a form of respect.

**10 BRAND IDENTITY** The specimen tag — 55×110mm, one corner cut at 45°, brass
grommet, three ruled hairlines, accession number in reject red.

**12 PHOTOGRAPHABLE** The rail: a hundred hand-written tags in a row, one of them
theirs, lit cold.

**13 TAKEAWAY** Your tag. You write the idea you're not over on it and decide whether
to leave it on the rail or take it home.

**14 COST** $$$. **15 COMPLEXITY** HIGH — the anonymous submission process is a
three-week job on its own.

**16 WHAT COULD GO WRONG** This is the concept's whole problem: it is one bad week from
being a public post-mortem. If a real reorg or cancellation is fresh, the wake stops
being funny and becomes the most tone-deaf party of the decade. It also requires the
host to be genuinely secure, and it hands a stakeholder an obvious reason to say no.

# 03 · PRESS ROW

**02 THE IDEA** The two hours are covered like a game — the party is the event, the
guests are the story, and everyone leaves holding the coverage.

**03 WHY IT WORKS** It flatters the room without complimenting it. Being photographed
seriously, on film, by someone good, is the single most reliable way to make people
stand up straighter and enjoy themselves.

**04 THE WORLD** Newsprint, ink, flash silver, one signal red used only for the record
dot. Low tungsten, hard practical pools, genuine intermittent flash. Scaffolding, gaffer
tape, exposed cable.

**05 THE HERO MOMENT** The film is processed in the back during the party. In the last
twenty minutes, wet-look prints go up on a wire and guests pull their own into a
glassine sleeve.

**08 MUSIC** Decks on a scaffold riser under a single clamp light. No booth facade.

**09 FOOD** Newsprint-lined aluminum trays, one-handed only, so nobody has to put a
drink down.

**10 BRAND IDENTITY** The timecode bar — a 12mm black rectangle, red record dot, white
mono elapsed time, always flush to an edge, always a different time.

**13 TAKEAWAY** Your own flash-lit black-and-white print, still curling.

**14 COST** $$$. **15 COMPLEXITY** HIGH.

**16 WHAT COULD GO WRONG** The live darkroom is a single point of failure with a
chemistry temperature problem, and it is also the hero moment — if it fails, the party
loses its ending at minute 100 with no substitute. It also risks a subtler failure: a
party *about* being photographed can make the shy third of the room feel hunted.
